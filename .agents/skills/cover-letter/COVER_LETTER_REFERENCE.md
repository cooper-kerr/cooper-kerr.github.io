# Cover Letter Reference

Use this reference when letter craft matters beyond source selection.

## Structure

Default to 3-5 concise paragraphs:

1. Opening hook: name the role and the specific reason Cooper fits the problem.
2. Evidence paragraph: connect two or three Cooper proof points to the job requirements.
3. Company or role fit: connect the role's mission, product, or domain to Cooper's actual interests and work.
4. Close: restate interest and invite a conversation.

For short recruiter notes or application text boxes, compress this into one or two paragraphs.

## Tone Options

- Conversational: best for startups, internships, recruiter messages, and short intros.
- Professional: best for formal applications and finance/corporate roles.
- Technical: best for data science, quant, ML, analytics, and engineering-adjacent roles.
- Mission-driven: best when the posting clearly emphasizes impact, policy, health, research, or education.

## Strong Openings

Prefer a concrete opening over a generic application sentence.

Cooper-specific patterns:

- `I am drawn to this role because it combines the two parts of data work I enjoy most: building reliable quantitative models and turning the results into decisions people can use.`
- `My background is a close match for this role's mix of product analytics, experimentation, and stakeholder communication. At PING Golf, I have been applying that mix to usage analysis, recommendation logic, and predictive modeling.`
- `The part of this role that stands out to me is the need to measure tradeoffs carefully. That is the kind of work I have done across PING's fitting systems, sports-science research, and a finance analytics platform built from real-time market data.`

## Paragraph Patterns

Evidence paragraph:

```markdown
At [context], I [work performed], which [result]. That experience maps directly to [job need] because it required [skills or judgment].
```

Company-fit paragraph:

```markdown
What interests me about [Company/team] is [specific company or role detail]. I would bring [Cooper strength] and a habit of [working style] to help [team outcome].
```

Referral paragraph:

```markdown
[Referrer] suggested I look into the [role] opening. After reading about the team, I was especially interested in [specific detail], because it connects closely to my work on [Cooper proof point].
```

Career-shift paragraph:

```markdown
Although my experience has been concentrated in [current domain], the work has trained the same muscles this role needs: [transferable skill], [transferable skill], and [transferable skill].
```

## Good Cover Letter Habits

- Add value beyond the resume; do not list every role.
- Use one clear through-line instead of several disconnected qualifications.
- Include company-specific claims only when supplied by the user or verified through current research.
- Keep the letter under one page unless the user asks for a long-form statement.
- Make the final paragraph simple and confident.
- Proofread for the company name, role title, dates, and any copied phrasing from a previous application.

## Common Weaknesses To Fix

- Generic enthusiasm with no evidence.
- Resume bullets pasted into paragraph form.
- Overclaiming direct experience when Cooper has adjacent experience.
- Too much focus on what Cooper wants instead of what he can help the team do.
- Inflated phrases such as `uniquely qualified`, `perfect fit`, or `passionate about leveraging synergies`.

## Example: Privacy-Safe Ads / Product Analytics

```markdown
Dear Hiring Manager,

I am excited to apply for the [Role] position because it sits at the intersection of product analytics, experimentation, and privacy-conscious measurement. I enjoy data work most when the question is not just what changed, but why it changed and what a product team should do next.

At PING Golf, I have analyzed API request volume and menu-usage patterns for PING Copilot before and after a redesign, identifying engagement shifts and turning them into findings for stakeholders across the Copilot ecosystem. I am also helping refactor PING's WebFit Fullbag recommendation logic for a site historically used about 40,000 times per day, and redesigned a predictive model for wedge launch conditions that reached 87% accuracy while reducing mean absolute error by roughly 40%.

That background maps well to this role's focus on targeting efficacy, signal loss, and measurement tradeoffs. I have worked with SQL, Python, R, dashboards, and applied statistical thinking across product, finance, and sports-science settings, including a University of Sydney platform that modeled 30,000+ options records daily and University of Utah analysis across 120 practices and games.

I would bring a quantitative background, a practical product mindset, and a careful communication style to your team. I would welcome the chance to discuss how my experience could support your work on privacy-safe monetization and resilient measurement.

Sincerely,
Cooper Kerr
```

Use the example as a craft model, not fixed text. Replace the role, company, and proof points according to the job posting.
